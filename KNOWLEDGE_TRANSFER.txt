================================================================================
1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

TruePixel is a silent, real-time identity verification and deepfake detection
desktop application written in Python. It runs in the background while an
employee is on a video call (Microsoft Teams, Zoom, etc.) and periodically
verifies that the person on camera is the real enrolled user — not a video
replay attack, photo, or sophisticated deepfake.

Built for Infosys employee laptops. Goal: pitch to leadership first, then
deploy as a Windows .exe to actual employees.

Core insight: identity (who) is necessary but not sufficient. A photo or video
replay also shows the right identity. We layer multiple LIVENESS signals on
top of identity matching, including physiological (heartbeat via rPPG),
behavioural (blinking, micro-movements), and spatial (face vs background
correlation). A composite score gates verification.

Current state: 35/35 unit tests pass. Real-world replay defence is empirically
marginal (best implementation achieves ~23% false-positive rate on real users
and ~44% false-negative rate on phone-screen replays). Multi-signal liveness
expansion is the current direction to improve those numbers.


================================================================================
2. THE PROBLEM
================================================================================

Original problem statement, expanded:

Infosys employees join video calls daily. There is no real-time mechanism to
verify that the person visible on camera is actually the employee — not:
  - A pre-recorded video of them being played on a phone screen
  - A photo printed or shown on a tablet
  - An AI-generated deepfake driven by a different person's audio
  - A coerced person being substituted in
  - An identity twin / look-alike

The cost of failed identity verification at Infosys: information leak,
unauthorised access to client material, fraudulent attendance, credential
abuse. Existing solutions (e.g. Microsoft Hello Face) require IR depth
hardware not present on most employee laptops. We need a software-only
solution that works on standard webcams.

Deployment target: Windows laptops (1920x1080 typical, fluorescent or window
lighting typical). Employee workflow must be uninterrupted — NO active
challenges ("blink now", "turn left"), NO popups, NO clicks. The app must
run silently in the background.

3. DEPLOYMENT CONTEXT

  - Then rollout to actual employees on production laptops

  - PyInstaller bundle target — single-folder Windows .exe
  - First-run downloads Facenet model weights (~92 MB) from GitHub.
    On corporate-VPN networks this download has historically been
    blocked or rate-limited (~14 minutes on first run).

Privacy posture:
  - No frames or biometric data leave the laptop
  - Enrolment photos and embeddings stored in data/enrolled/<name>/ locally
  - No telemetry to a server

Architecture posture:
  - Three independent detector classes that can be calibrated, replaced, or
    augmented without touching each other
  - Single source of truth for tunable constants (config.py)
  - Composite scoring with documented attack-surface analysis for each weight

================================================================================
4. ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

The pipeline runs three parallel concerns:

    Camera (webcam, background thread, continuous)
        |
        +-- FaceMesh overlay (every frame, lightweight)
        |       +-- Dashboard live feed
        |
        +-- VerificationScheduler (background thread)
                |
                +-- Wait for next check interval
                |     TEST_MODE: every 10 seconds
                |     PRODUCTION: 2min -> 5min -> 10min -> 15-20min random
                |
                +-- BURST CAPTURE (8 seconds, 15 fps, ~120 frames)
                        |
                        +-- FaceMatcher (every 5th frame):
                        |     DeepFace + Facenet/Facenet512 embeddings
                        |     Multi-pose enrolment matching
                        |     -> face_confidence (0.0-1.0)
                        |
                        +-- BlinkDetector (every frame):
                        |     EAR algorithm
                        |     Plus: iris pos, nose pos, mouth shape tracking
                        |     -> blink rate, EAR variance, micro-movement metrics
                        |
                        +-- RPPGDetector (every frame):
                        |     Green-channel forehead ROI
                        |     + 4 face-adjacent BG patches
                        |     -> heart rate (BPM) via Butterworth + FFT
                        |     -> signal_quality (peak/total power ratio)
                        |     -> spectral_replay_suspected (PPGSecure-inspired)
                        |     -> background_correlation (legacy Pearson, diagnostic)
                        |
                        +-- Composite scoring:
                              composite = round(
                                face_conf * 0.50
                              + blink_score * 0.30
                              + max(0, rPPG_quality - NOISE_FLOOR) * 0.20,
                                4
                              )
                              |
                              +-- if spectral_replay_suspected AND frames >= 60
                              |     -> force FAIL ("Replay attack suspected")
                              +-- if composite >= 0.70 -> PASS (VERIFIED)
                              +-- else -> FAIL (alert only on 2 consecutive fails)


Pipeline characteristics:
  - Camera runs continuously, never blocks the UI
  - Face mesh overlay is cheap, runs every frame to make the live feed feel real
  - Heavy detectors only run during burst capture (every 10s in test, every
    5-20m in production)
  - Burst capture grabs frames into a list, then detectors run sequentially
  - Single PASS/FAIL verdict per burst, not per frame
  - Consecutive-fail rule: a single fail is normal (user glanced at phone),
    only escalate to user-visible ALERT after 2 consecutive fails


================================================================================
5. COMPONENTS IN DETAIL
================================================================================

5.1 core/camera.py — webcam I/O
    Threaded webcam wrapper. Camera.start() opens cv2.VideoCapture, spawns
    a background thread that continuously reads frames into self._frame
    (protected by self._lock). Camera.get_frame() returns a copy of the
    latest frame from any caller. Camera.stop() releases the device cleanly.

    Designed so the UI and the scheduler never block on camera reads.


5.2 core/face_mesh.py — MediaPipe wrapper
    Wraps mediapipe.solutions.face_mesh.FaceMesh. Each frame returns:
      - 468 facial landmarks as a list of (x, y) pixel tuples
      - An annotated frame with mesh drawn for the dashboard

    IMPORTANT VERSION CONSTRAINT: file has a top-level assertion that
    mediapipe.__version__ <= "0.10.21". Newer mediapipe removed the
    mp.solutions.* namespace this code uses. If pip ever upgrades mediapipe,
    that assert fires immediately and the app refuses to start. Pinning is
    enforced in requirements.txt at mediapipe==0.10.9.


5.3 core/face_matcher.py — identity verification (DeepFace + Facenet)
    Pre-loads the configured DeepFace model at startup (warm-up). Two stages:

    ENROLMENT:
      - Takes 6 pose/condition-varied photos (NOT 3; lesson learned from
        deployment — single-pose enrolment dropped cosine similarity by
        0.20+ when the user was backlit or slouched)
      - Each photo's embedding is stored separately (NOT averaged) in
        data/enrolled/<name>/embeddings.npy as an (N, embed_dim) matrix
      - Photos saved alongside as photo_1.jpg .. photo_6.jpg
      - Backward-compatible with legacy single-vector embedding.npy

    MATCHING:
      - On a live frame, extract one embedding via the same DeepFace model
      - Compute cosine similarity against EACH enrolled embedding
      - Take the MEAN (not MAX) — MEAN prevents a stranger who happens to
        match ONE enrolled pose from scoring high
      - Compare against config.FACE_MATCH_THRESHOLD

    MODEL SWAP SUPPORT:
      - config.FACE_RECOGNITION_MODEL controls which DeepFace model to use
        (e.g. "Facenet" 128-dim or "Facenet512" 512-dim)
      - config.FACE_EMBEDDING_DIM declares the expected dimension
      - On startup, if saved embeddings don't match the current model's
        dimension, _re_extract_embeddings_from_photos() runs the new
        model over the saved enrolment JPEGs and overwrites the .npy
        files. User does NOT have to re-enrol manually after a model swap.


5.4 core/blink_detector.py — EAR + multi-signal liveness
    Two responsibilities:

    BLINK DETECTION (original):
      - 6 landmarks per eye -> Eye Aspect Ratio formula
      - When EAR drops below EAR_THRESHOLD for BLINK_CONSEC_FRAMES,
        a blink is confirmed
      - Maintains a rolling 60-second deque of blink timestamps
      - Burst-scoped count tracked separately

    MULTI-SIGNAL LIVENESS (expanded):
      Designed because a static photo has every behavioural signal == 0,
      but a real human always has at least one. Currently tracks:
        - Burst blink count (from EAR)
        - EAR variance across the burst (eye micro-movements)
        - Iris-center position trajectory (gaze drift)
        - Nose-tip position trajectory (head sway)
        - Mouth height / width over time (subtle expression changes)

      compute_liveness_score() aggregates these into a single 0.0-1.0
      score. A static photo: all five signals near zero. A live person:
      at least 2-3 of them registering activity.

      All buffers cleared per burst via reset().

    Requires the following landmark indices from config.py:
      - LEFT_EYE_INDICES, RIGHT_EYE_INDICES (6 each)
      - IRIS_LEFT_CENTER (iris tracking from refined landmarks)
      - NOSE_TIP_LANDMARK
      - MOUTH_TOP_LIP_CENTER, MOUTH_BOTTOM_LIP_CENTER,
        MOUTH_LEFT_CORNER, MOUTH_RIGHT_CORNER


5.5 core/rppg_detector.py — heartbeat + replay defence
    Three concerns folded into one detector:

    HEARTBEAT (rPPG, Wang et al. MIT 2016):
      - Forehead ROI from FOREHEAD_LANDMARKS
      - Mean green-channel value per frame -> rolling 8s buffer
      - 3rd-order Butterworth bandpass filter 0.75-2.5 Hz (45-150 BPM)
      - FFT, find dominant frequency in band -> BPM
      - signal_quality = peak power / total band power (0.0-1.0)

    REPLAY DEFENCE (PPGSecure-inspired, Nowara et al. IEEE FG 2017):
      - Samples 4 patches ADJACENT to face bounding box (top/bottom/left/right)
      - Each patch gets its own rolling green-channel buffer
      - PRIMARY check: is_spectral_replay_suspected()
          * Band-limited FFT of face buffer
          * Same for each BG patch
          * Replay flagged if any BG patch has a peak within
            REPLAY_SPECTRAL_FREQ_TOLERANCE_HZ of the face peak,
            AND with power >= REPLAY_SPECTRAL_MIN_POWER_RATIO of face peak
      - SECONDARY (deprecated, diagnostic only):
          compute_face_background_correlation() — legacy MAX-over-patches
          Pearson correlation. Threshold set to 0.99 = effectively disabled.
          Kept for log diagnostics.

    BUFFER MANAGEMENT:
      - All buffers (green forehead + 4 BG patches) cleared on reset()
        which is called at the start of each burst
      - This prevents bursts from contaminating each other

    LIVE VISUALISATION:
      - start_live_graph(camera, face_mesh) opens a separate matplotlib
        window for validation against a phone health app
      - RPPGGraphPanel in dashboard embeds the same view inline


5.6 core/burst_capture.py — windowed frame collection
    Simple helper. BurstCapture.capture() reads frames at target_fps for
    duration_seconds and returns the list. Used by the scheduler at each
    verification cycle.


5.7 core/verification_scheduler.py — orchestration + composite scoring
    The brain. Holds a reference to all 5 detectors. Public API:
      - on_check_complete(callback): fires after every check
      - on_alert(callback): fires only on 2 consecutive failed checks
      - on_burst_start(callback): UI hint that burst is running
      - start() / stop()
      - time_until_next_check()

    Internal scheduling loop wakes every 5 seconds. When the next-check
    time has passed, _run_check() executes:
      1. Notify UI burst is starting
      2. Reset blink_detector + rppg_detector buffers
      3. burst_capture.capture() -> list of frames
      4. For each frame: run face_mesh, face_matcher (every 5th), blink, rPPG
      5. Aggregate: average face confidence, extrapolate blink count to
         per-minute rate, take LAST rPPG result (FFT improves as buffer fills)
      6. Compute composite score with noise-floor clamp on rPPG
      7. Check spectral replay flag — overrides PASS
      8. Build CheckResult dataclass, fire callbacks
      9. Advance to next phase, schedule next check

    Composite formula:
      composite = round(
        face_conf * FACE_MATCH_WEIGHT +     # 0.50
        blink_score * BLINK_WEIGHT +         # 0.30
        max(0, rPPG_quality - NOISE_FLOOR) * HEARTBEAT_WEIGHT,   # 0.20
        4
      )

    Composite passes if composite >= COMPOSITE_ALERT_THRESHOLD (0.70).
    BUT if spectral_replay_suspected is True with >= 60 frames captured,
    passed is forced to False and the alert_reason explains why.


5.8 ui/dashboard.py — Tkinter window
    Two-section layout:
      TOP (1100x520):
        - Left: live video feed (640x480) with face mesh overlay
        - Right: status panels (Identity, Confidence, Blink rate, EAR,
          Heart rate, Signal quality, Event Log)
      BOTTOM (1100x480):
        - Embedded 3-panel matplotlib rPPG live signal graph
          (raw green, bandpass filtered, BPM scatter over time)

    Methods are thread-safe via root.after(0, ...) dispatch:
      - update_frame(frame)
      - update_status(data)
      - update_rppg_graph(detector)
      - show_alert(message) / clear_alert()
      - show_burst_indicator(active)
      - update_countdown(seconds)


5.9 ui/components.py — reusable widgets
    - StatusIndicator: big VERIFIED / ALERT / INITIALIZING label
      with wraplength so long messages don't clip
    - MetricRow: label + colour-coded value pair
    - LogPanel: scrollable Text widget with timestamped entries
    - RPPGGraphPanel: FigureCanvasTkAgg-embedded matplotlib panel


5.10 core/logger_setup.py — UTF-8 logging
    Centralised logging. Writes to BOTH console AND a rotating file at
    logs/truepixel_YYYYMMDD.log. The console handler wraps sys.stderr.buffer
    in a UTF-8 io.TextIOWrapper because Windows PowerShell defaults to cp1252
    which can't encode characters like ✓ ✗ that we use in log messages.
    Without this wrap, Python's logging.StreamHandler would raise
    UnicodeEncodeError mid-log.

7.1 Day 1 — scaffold and dependency hell (~5 hours)
    - Built skeleton project + stub detectors
    - Solved mediapipe API removal (0.10.9 pin)
    - Solved TF 2.21 protobuf 6 vs mediapipe protobuf 3 conflict (TF 2.13.1)

    - Solved numpy 1.24.4 violating TF 2.13's <=1.24.3 (fixed to 1.24.3)
    - Established the one-shot install method
    - Built scripts/verify_env.py for portable sanity checks

7.2 Day 2 — blink detector
    - EAR algorithm per Soukupova & Cech 2016
    - Calibrated for screen-work deployment context:
        Medical lit: focused screen work = 5-10 blinks/min
        Default human resting = 15-20 blinks/min
        Range adopted: 5-30/min full credit, 2+ minimal credit, <2 fail
    - Bugs caught:
        BlinkDetector.reset() was never wired into the scheduler
        Per-frame blink_rate was being averaged across the burst
          instead of being an extrapolated per-minute count
        Division-by-zero on BURST_DURATION_SECONDS

7.3 Day 3 — rPPG heartbeat detection
    - Wang et al. MIT 2016 rPPG algorithm
    - Forehead ROI -> green channel mean -> Butterworth bandpass -> FFT
    - Bug caught: RPPG_SAMPLE_RATE was 30 (from Day 1 before burst mode
      existed). Buffer expected 240 samples but burst delivered only 120
      at 15 fps -> 50% fill -> FFT never executed.
      Fix: RPPG_SAMPLE_RATE = BURST_FPS = 15.
      Added a coupling integration test to lock the invariant.
    - Live signal graph embedded in dashboard

7.4 Composite weight rebalance for office lighting
    - Original equal weights (0.40 / 0.30 / 0.30) couldn't push composite
      over 0.70 with low rPPG (real-world quality 0.10-0.16 in mixed office)
    - Rebalanced to 0.50 / 0.30 / 0.20
    - Attack-surface analysis comments added to config.py

7.5 Video replay attack discovery (CRITICAL FINDING)
    - User-discovered attack: held phone playing video of themselves in
      front of webcam. System VERIFIED them at composite 0.72.
    - This is the canonical real-world attack against face biometric
      systems. We had no defence.
    - Two defences added:
        a) rPPG_NOISE_FLOOR = 0.15 (later 0.12): rppg_contribution =
           max(0.0, quality - NOISE_FLOOR). Phone screen artefacts in
           range 0.10-0.15 contribute zero.
        b) face-bg correlation check (PPGSecure-inspired)

7.6 Replay defence calibration attempts (the hard part)
    Multiple attempts to make face-background correlation work in real
    office conditions. All struggled because:
      - Fluorescent lighting at 50/60 Hz aliases through CMOS rolling
        shutter to produce correlated noise across face and BG regions
      - Background human motion correlates spuriously with face signal
      - Phone bezels and hands are mostly static in face-adjacent regions,
        so phone replays don't always produce strong correlation
      - rPPG signals are fundamentally low-SNR; small noise sources
        dominate
    Empirical results across attempts:
      Single top-left ROI:           never triggered (always too flat)
      Face-adjacent Pearson (Path B): 40% FP / 41% FN
      FFT spectral peak (Path C):    23% FP / 44% FN
    No rPPG-only replay defence reached production-grade reliability
    in this office environment.

7.7 Multi-pose enrolment expansion
    - Single-pose / 3-photo enrolment was a studio-style clean baseline
      that didn't survive real conditions:
        Backlit by a window: cosine dropped 0.20+
        Slumped posture: dropped another 0.10
        Earphones: dropped another 0.05
    - Expanded to 6 photos with intentional pose/condition variation
    - Each photo's embedding stored SEPARATELY (not averaged)
    - At match time: MEAN cosine across all enrolled poses (not MAX)
    - Auto re-extraction when embedding dimension doesn't match current model

7.8 Multi-signal liveness expansion
    - Beyond just blinks. Static photos have all 5 signals = 0;
      real humans have at least 2-3 active.
    - BlinkDetector now also tracks:
        EAR variance (eye micro-movements)
        Iris center position trajectory (gaze drift)
        Nose-tip position trajectory (head sway)
        Mouth height / width over time (subtle expression changes)
    - compute_liveness_score() aggregates into single 0.0-1.0

11. KNOWN ISSUES AND LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

11.1 Replay defence is marginal in real-world office conditions
    - Per-check false-positive rate: 23%
    - Per-check false-negative rate: 44%
    - Consecutive-fail rule (need 2 in a row) reduces user-visible
      false alerts to ~5% under independence assumption
    - But sustained replay attack can succeed by chance with non-trivial
      probability
    - Root cause: rPPG signals are inherently low-SNR; fluorescent
      light flicker and CMOS rolling-shutter aliasing produce similar
      statistical signatures to phone screen flicker
    - Path forward (not yet implemented): LBP texture detection as
      Layer 2 defence — completely independent signal that detects
      the difference between skin (porous, textured) and screens
      (uniform pixels)

11.2 Real-world pass rate sensitivity to rPPG quality
    - In mixed office lighting, rPPG signal_quality often drops below
      0.15 noise floor for real users
    - When clamped to 0, composite caps at face*0.50 + blink*0.30 = ~0.70
    - User glances away or breathes irregularly -> rPPG drops more ->
      composite falls just below threshold -> FAIL
    - Tolerated by consecutive-fail rule, but not ideal UX
    - Improvement candidates:
        Move some weight from rPPG back to blink/face if rPPG repeatedly
        below floor for this user (per-user calibration)
        Add multi-signal liveness aggregation to composite (already
        scaffolded in blink_detector.py, not yet wired in)

11.3 Out-of-scope attacks (documented, not defended)
    - 3D-printed mask: requires IR depth sensor (no hardware)
    - High-quality deepfake with synthetic blood flow: research frontier
    - Identical twin or convincing sibling: face match would pass both
    - USB camera-feed injection: software cannot defend against drivers
    - Coercion / under duress: no anti-coercion signal exists
    - User wearing sunglasses or face mask: blink/EAR fail

11.4 Demo readiness concerns
    - Live "verify me" demo will sometimes false-fail due to issue 11.2
    - Live "replay attack defence" demo will sometimes false-pass
      due to issue 11.1
    - Demo script should rely on architecture explanation + math walkthrough
      more than live theatrics; reserve live demo for the photo attack
      (which always fails correctly) and identity match (which works well)

11.5 First-run network requirement
    - DeepFace downloads ~92 MB Facenet weights from GitHub on first
      use of FaceMatcher().enroll()
    - Behind corporate VPN this can fail or be very slow
    - Workaround: manually download facenet_weights.h5 from
      https://github.com/serengil/deepface_models/releases
      and place it in ~/.deepface/weights/

14. GLOSSARY
================================================================================

rPPG (remote photoplethysmography):
    Detecting heart rate from subtle skin colour changes in video.
    A real face has blood flow that subtly modulates green-channel
    intensity at the heartbeat frequency. A photo or screen does not.

EAR (Eye Aspect Ratio):
    A geometric ratio of vertical to horizontal eye landmark distances.
    Drops sharply (below ~0.20) during a blink. Used to count blinks.

PPGSecure:
    Anti-spoofing approach from Nowara et al. (IEEE FG 2017). Uses rPPG
    signal extracted from both face and background regions. A real face
    has pulse in face but not background; a phone replay has the screen's
    pulsing affecting both. Comparing them distinguishes the two.

LBP (Local Binary Pattern):
    Texture descriptor that captures local pixel-level variation. Real
    skin has high LBP variance (pores, hairs, micro-features); phone
    screens have near-zero LBP variance (uniform pixels).

Burst capture:
    The 8-second window during which all heavy detectors run. Picked
    because rPPG needs at least 8 seconds of frames to fill its FFT
    buffer.

Composite score:
    Weighted sum of face_confidence, blink_score, and rPPG_contribution.
    The single number that gates PASS/FAIL.

Noise floor:
    Minimum rPPG signal quality to count toward composite. Quality below
    floor contributes zero (clamped at floor). Designed to reject phone
    screen artefacts which sit in the 0.05-0.15 range.

Face-adjacent patches:
    4 small regions sampled immediately around the face bounding box
    (top, bottom, left, right). Used for PPGSecure replay defence.

Consecutive-fail rule:
    A single failed check does not fire an ALERT to the user. Only
    after 2 consecutive failures does the system alert. Masks transient
    false positives from a glance away.

Multi-pose enrolment:
    Enrolment captures 6 photos with intentional pose/condition variation
    (frontal, side, looking down, backlit, etc.). Each photo's embedding
    is stored separately. Match uses MEAN cosine similarity across all
    poses.

TEST_MODE / PRODUCTION mode:
    TEST_MODE = True runs verifications every 10 seconds for dev/demo.
    PRODUCTION mode uses an adaptive 2/5/10/15-20-minute phase ladder.
