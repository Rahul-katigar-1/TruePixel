================================================================================
TRUEPIXEL — PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================================

A focused statement of the problem TruePixel exists to solve.
For implementation details and current architecture see KNOWLEDGE_TRANSFER.txt.
For chronological development log see PROGRESS.md.


================================================================================
1. CONTEXT
================================================================================

Infosys employees join video calls daily over Microsoft Teams, Zoom, and
similar platforms. These calls are the primary medium for:

  - Client meetings (where confidential project material is discussed)
  - Internal stand-ups, design reviews, and approvals
  - Training and certifications (attendance is recorded)
  - Performance reviews and HR discussions
  - Access requests where verbal authorisation grants permissions

In every one of these scenarios, the system assumes the person visible on
camera is who they claim to be. Today, that assumption is unverified. The
laptop's webcam shows a face; Teams shows a name next to it; the meeting
proceeds.

There is no real-time mechanism on a standard employee laptop to confirm
the live face on camera matches the enrolled employee — and is a real
human, not a recording or a digital impersonation.


================================================================================
2. THE PROBLEM
================================================================================

A bad actor can defeat current video-call identity assumptions using
techniques that require no special equipment:

2.1 Video replay attack
    A pre-recorded video of an employee (sourced from social media, a
    previous internal meeting recording, or a covert capture) played back
    on a phone or tablet held in front of the webcam.

2.2 Photo / printed attack
    A high-resolution photograph of an employee, printed or shown on a
    second screen, held in front of the webcam.

2.3 Deepfake driven by a different person
    An AI-generated face swap controlled by an attacker's audio/video,
    rendered in real time on the webcam.

2.4 Substitution under coercion
    A trusted person is replaced or coerced into participating in a
    meeting on someone else's behalf.

2.5 Identity confusion / look-alikes
    A close relative or look-alike participates instead of the enrolled
    employee, possibly with consent of that employee.

In all five cases the existing video-call platform treats the imposter
as the legitimate employee. There is no signal that anything is wrong.


================================================================================
3. WHY THIS MATTERS (BUSINESS IMPACT)
================================================================================

The cost of unverified identity in employee video calls falls on Infosys
in several distinct ways:

3.1 Information leakage
    Client material discussed in meetings reaches an unauthorised party
    when an imposter joins. Depending on the client, this may breach NDAs
    or contractual confidentiality obligations and result in financial
    penalties or loss of the account.

3.2 Fraudulent attendance and credentialing
    Training sessions, certifications, and performance reviews assume the
    attendee is the named person. Successful impersonation produces
    credentials and HR records that do not reflect reality.

3.3 Unauthorised access via verbal approval
    Production systems, client data, and elevated permissions are routinely
    granted on the basis of "I see you on the call, you're approved." An
    imposter on a call can extract those approvals.

3.4 Audit and compliance failure
    Regulated client work (banking, healthcare, government) requires
    verifiable participant identity. Failure to demonstrate verification
    is itself a compliance gap, separately from any actual breach.

3.5 Trust erosion if exploited at scale
    A single successful demonstration that the system can be defeated
    undermines trust in every video-mediated approval and decision Infosys
    relies on. The cost is not just the incident but the chilling effect
    on remote workflows.


================================================================================
4. WHO IS AFFECTED
================================================================================

Primary stakeholders (gain protection if the problem is solved):

  - Infosys employees, whose identity is currently impersonable without
    their knowledge or consent
  - Infosys clients, whose confidential material is exposed when employees
    can be impersonated in client-facing meetings
  - Infosys leadership and compliance teams, who carry the audit risk

Secondary stakeholders (must accept the solution into their workflow):

  - Infosys IT department, who must deploy and support the solution on
    every employee laptop without breaking existing software
  - Infosys employees again, this time in their day-to-day capacity:
    the solution must NOT interrupt them, prompt them, or visibly slow
    their machine

Adversaries (intentionally not stakeholders but must be understood):

  - External attackers with social-engineering access to employee photos
    or recorded videos
  - Insiders attempting impersonation for fraud or harassment
  - Curious employees who learn an exploit exists and share it informally


================================================================================
5. CURRENT STATE OF SOLUTIONS
================================================================================

5.1 What exists in the market today
    Apple Face ID, Microsoft Windows Hello, and bank-app face login all
    perform live face verification. They work — but they rely on hardware
    not present on standard Infosys laptops:

      - IR depth sensors (Face ID, Hello)
      - Time-of-flight cameras (high-end smartphones)
      - Dual-camera stereoscopic depth

    Without specialised hardware, every commercial face-verification
    product becomes vulnerable to the same replay/photo/deepfake attacks
    described in Section 2.

5.2 What does NOT exist
    A software-only, real-time, passive identity verification layer that:
      - Runs alongside any video-call client
      - Uses only the standard webcam already on the laptop
      - Requires no user interaction during calls
      - Continuously monitors for impersonation rather than checking once
        at login

5.3 Why nobody has shipped this yet
    The technical challenge is real: distinguishing a live face from a
    high-quality recording on a webcam, using only software, in arbitrary
    office lighting, is at the frontier of computer-vision anti-spoofing
    research. The methods exist in academic papers (rPPG, LBP texture
    analysis, PPGSecure, FAS-CNN) but no productised tool combines them
    into a deployment-ready desktop application aligned to a corporate
    deployment context.


================================================================================
6. DESIRED OUTCOME
================================================================================

A desktop application that runs silently on every Infosys employee laptop
and continuously verifies, while any video call is active, that:

  6.1 The face on camera matches the enrolled employee's face
      (identity verification — who is this?)

  6.2 The face on camera belongs to a living human, not a recording
      (liveness verification — is this actually happening right now?)

  6.3 The verification happens passively, with no impact on the
      employee's workflow — no challenges, no popups, no waiting

  6.4 Failed verification produces a clear audit log entry (timestamped,
      with a confidence-broken-down explanation), allowing IT and
      compliance to investigate without requiring the employee to do
      anything

  6.5 The system has known, documented failure modes and limitations,
      and degrades gracefully under attack (it should never silently
      pass an attack OR silently lock out a legitimate user)


================================================================================
7. CONSTRAINTS
================================================================================

Deployment constraints that any solution must accept:

  7.1 Standard hardware only
      Webcam, CPU, RGB only. No assumption of IR camera, depth sensor,
      external attachments, or USB peripherals.

  7.2 Standard operating system
      Windows 10 / 11 on a corporate laptop. No admin-level installation
      that would require IT to whitelist every machine.

  7.3 No interruption to employee workflow
      Cannot prompt the user to do anything during verification. No
      "blink now", no "turn your head", no "click to confirm". The app
      must be invisible to the employee unless an attack is detected.

  7.4 No network dependency at runtime
      Frames and biometric data must not leave the laptop. Model weights
      may download on first install, but the verification loop must
      work offline.

  7.5 Reasonable resource budget
      The laptop must remain usable for Teams calls, browsers, IDEs.
      Continuous CPU usage above ~10% in idle state is too much. Memory
      footprint should stay below ~1 GB.

  7.6 Acceptable failure modes
      False positives (locking out a legitimate user) and false negatives
      (allowing an attack) are both bad, but in this product:
        - False positives must be rare and recoverable. A locked-out
          legitimate user files a help-desk ticket; this must not be
          a daily occurrence.
        - False negatives must be reduced as far as practical, with
          remaining gaps openly documented and accepted as the cost of
          a software-only solution.

  7.7 Reproducible deployment
      The same .exe must work on a freshly-imaged Infosys laptop
      without per-machine tuning. Calibration variations (skin tone,
      lighting, webcam model) must be handled by the software, not by
      IT during install.


================================================================================
8. SUCCESS CRITERIA
================================================================================

The solution will be considered successful when:

  8.1 Daily false-positive rate on real employees is below 1 per shift
      (i.e., an employee on an 8-hour day sees at most 1 false alert)

  8.2 Photo / printed-image attacks fail in 100% of test cases

  8.3 Phone-screen video replay attacks fail in at least 90% of test
      cases, across 3 different office lighting conditions

  8.4 Deepfake replay attacks fail in at least 60% of test cases against
      a representative consumer-grade deepfake (no claim made against
      research-frontier deepfakes)

  8.5 No employee complaint of perceptible system slowdown caused by
      TruePixel during normal video-call usage

  8.6 The .exe installs cleanly on a freshly-imaged Infosys laptop in
      under 10 minutes with no IT intervention

  8.7 All detection events are logged locally with sufficient detail for
      a compliance officer to reconstruct what was seen and why a verdict
      was reached, without any frames being stored


================================================================================
9. OUT OF SCOPE (KNOWN LIMITS — INTENTIONAL)
================================================================================

The following are NOT goals of this build. Listed so they cannot be
assumed and then later treated as failures:

  9.1 Defence against 3D-printed face masks
      Requires depth-sensing hardware not present on laptops.

  9.2 Defence against high-quality research-grade deepfakes with
      synthetic blood-flow simulation
      Open research problem; even commercial systems do not solve it.

  9.3 Defence against identical twins or convincing siblings
      Without a second factor (voice, badge, password), this is not
      solvable with face biometrics alone.

  9.4 Defence against USB-level camera-feed injection
      A malicious driver can replace the camera output entirely. Software
      cannot detect this from inside the same machine.

  9.5 Defence against coercion or under-duress impersonation
      No anti-coercion signal exists in software. Out of scope unless
      paired with a separate workflow (panic-word, dual-approval).

  9.6 Defence when the employee is wearing sunglasses or a face covering
      Blink and EAR signals are blocked; face match degrades. The system
      will refuse to verify rather than guess.

  9.7 General real-time deepfake detection on arbitrary video streams
      TruePixel verifies one specific enrolled identity per laptop. It is
      not a generic deepfake-detection product.

10. ROADMAP — PROTOTYPE TODAY VS PRODUCT WITH RESOURCES
================================================================================

This section is for the leadership / jury pitch. Be explicit about what
TruePixel IS today and what it can BECOME with investment.

10.1 What TruePixel IS today (working prototype)
    - End-to-end working desktop application running on a real Infosys
      laptop
    - Uses PRE-TRAINED, off-the-shelf computer vision models:
        * Facenet512 (DeepFace) for face identity verification
        * MediaPipe Face Mesh (Google) for facial landmark detection
        * POS algorithm (Wang et al. 2017) for heart-rate signal extraction
        * EAR algorithm (Soukupova & Cech 2016) for blink detection
        * NLMS adaptive filter (classic DSP) for fluorescent flicker removal
        * Custom multi-signal liveness aggregation built on top
    - Architecture proven: identity + multi-signal liveness fused into
      a single composite verdict, with 60+ unit tests covering every
      detector and integration point
    - Real-world validation: prototype tested on actual office lighting,
      with measured false-positive and false-negative rates documented
    - Demonstrates the core thesis: software-only continuous identity
      and liveness verification IS achievable on standard enterprise
      hardware

10.2 What TruePixel CAN BECOME (with resources for training)
    The prototype's limits come from using GENERIC pre-trained models
    that were trained on public datasets from other contexts. With the
    following investments, TruePixel becomes a defensible product:

    A. Dataset collection (500-2000 Infosys employees, 1 quarter)
        - Diverse skin-tone coverage (Fitzpatrick I-VI), gender,
          age, accessory variations (glasses, earphones, beard)
        - Real-world office and home-office lighting captured directly,
          not simulated
        - Genuine vs spoof samples covering photo, screen replay, mask,
          and deepfake attack classes
        - Consent and data-handling under DPDP Act 2023 framework

    B. Custom model training (1 ML engineer, 1 quarter)
        - Replace pre-trained Facenet with an Infosys-fine-tuned face
          identity model trained on the captured employee dataset
        - Train a dedicated spatial anti-spoofing model (MiniFASNetV2
          or comparable) on the captured spoof set instead of relying
          on synthetic Moire augmentation
        - Tune the rPPG, blink, and liveness detectors against ground-
          truth labels rather than published thresholds

    C. Deployment readiness (1 IT/security engineer, ~6 weeks)
        - PyInstaller .exe bundling with all model weights embedded
        - IT-managed silent deployment to employee laptops
        - Encrypted local audit log integration with Infosys SIEM
        - Driver-level camera-injection defence using OS Media
          Foundation enumeration + frame-jitter analysis

10.3 Expected impact of investment
    Today (prototype):       Catches obvious attacks (photos, zero-blink
                             deepfakes, low-quality replays). Marginal
                             on sophisticated phone-screen replays in
                             fluorescent office lighting.

    With investment above:   Production-grade accuracy aligned to
                             Infosys-specific deployment conditions.
                             Expected to be within 1-2% of best-in-
                             class commercial anti-spoofing systems —
                             which require IR depth hardware that
                             Infosys laptops do not have.

10.4 Honest framing for the jury / leadership
    TruePixel is NOT a finished product today. It is a credible
    working demonstration that the problem can be solved with software
    alone on standard hardware. The hard part — the architecture, the
    multi-signal fusion, the algorithm selection, the threat-model
    discipline — is built and tested. What remains is the part every
    serious AI/ML product needs: a custom training pipeline, an
    Infosys-specific dataset, and the engineering polish for a
    production rollout.

    The decision in front of leadership is not "does this approach
    work" (it does — see the demo) but "is this approach worth
    investing in" (we believe yes — see the cost/benefit analysis
    in Section 3).


================================================================================
11. SUMMARY
================================================================================

Infosys video calls assume identity. Software cannot currently verify it
on standard employee laptops. Imposters can join calls, extract client
material, fraudulently obtain credentials, and abuse verbally-granted
approvals — with no signal that anything is wrong.

TruePixel is the software-only, passive, real-time identity-and-liveness
verification layer that closes this gap. It runs silently in the
background while employees work, continuously checks that the live face
on camera is the real enrolled employee, and produces an auditable trail
of every check.

It accepts that some attacks (3D masks, top-tier deepfakes, identical
twins, driver-level injection) are beyond what a software-only RGB-only
solution can defeat. The remaining envelope — replay attacks, photos,
consumer deepfakes — is large, common, and worth defending against.
